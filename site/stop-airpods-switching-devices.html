Mac guide · iOS 18 and macOS 15 Sequoia

AirPods keep switching devices? Make them stay put.

Automatic switching moves your AirPods to whichever Apple device starts playing sound. Turn it off on each device, and they stay with the one you connected last.

The short version: on each device, open your AirPods' settings and set Connect to This iPhone (or Mac, or iPad) to When Last Connected. Change it back to Automatically any time.

On your iPhone or iPad

  1. Put your AirPods in and connect them.The setting only shows while they're connected.
  2. Open their settings.Open Settings and tap your AirPods' name near the top. If you don't see it, tap Bluetooth, then the info button next to them.
  3. Choose When Last Connected.Tap Audio & Routing if you see it, then Connect to This iPhone, and choose When Last Connected to This iPhone.

On your Mac

  1. Put your AirPods in and connect them.Again, the option only appears while they're connected to this Mac.
  2. Open their settings.Choose Apple menu › System Settings and click your AirPods' name in the sidebar. Or click Bluetooth, then the info button next to your AirPods.
  3. Choose When Last Connected.Click Audio & Routing if you see it, then set Connect to This Mac to When Last Connected to This Mac.

Do this on every device you don't want your AirPods jumping to. The setting doesn't sync between them.

What Apple says about switching

  • Which AirPods do it. AirPods (2nd generation) and later, every AirPods Pro, and every AirPods Max.
  • Which devices. Ones signed in to the same Apple Account with two-factor authentication, on current software. On macOS Sonoma, your Mac asks before it takes your AirPods. Sequoia and later switch without asking.
  • Calls don't switch. Sound shouldn't move during a phone call, FaceTime call, or video conference. You can still switch by hand.

Your Mac switches your mic too

Stopping the AirPods from switching doesn't stop your Mac from switching. When AirPods connect, macOS makes them your sound and your mic. Using their mic drops them into call mode, so you sound like you're on a phone line, and the USB mic on your desk sits unused. An iPhone nearby can take over your camera the same way, through Continuity Camera.

macOS has no setting for a default mic or speaker that sticks. It goes with whatever connected last. To put it back by hand, open System Settings › Sound and pick your mic under Input and your speakers under Output. See AirPods mic not working for call mode.

Keep your Mac on the devices you choose

Green Room is a free menu bar app with a Priority list in its settings. Rank your mics, speakers, and cameras, and when something connects or disconnects, Green Room switches to the highest one that's plugged in.

  • AirPods don't take your mic. Rank your USB mic or the MacBook's own above them. You still hear through the AirPods, without call mode.
  • Unplug a mic, get your next choice. Not whatever macOS picked last.
  • Your iPhone stops grabbing the camera. Put your webcam above it.

A device you pick yourself stays until the next change. Free, with no account.

Questions

Why do my AirPods keep switching between my iPhone and Mac?

Automatic switching is on. AirPods follow whichever device signed in to your Apple Account starts playing sound. Set Connect to This iPhone and Connect to This Mac to When Last Connected to stop it.

Do I have to change the setting on every device?

Yes. The setting is per device. Change it on each iPhone, iPad, and Mac you don't want your AirPods jumping to.

Will my AirPods switch during a call?

Apple says sound shouldn't switch automatically during a phone call, FaceTime call, or video conference. You can still switch by hand.

Can I set a default microphone on my Mac that sticks?

macOS has no setting for it. It moves to the device that connected last. Green Room's Priority list keeps your Mac on the mic, speaker, and camera you rank highest.

Which AirPods switch automatically?

Apple lists AirPods (2nd generation) and later, every AirPods Pro, and every AirPods Max. All your devices need to be signed in to the same Apple Account with two-factor authentication.
