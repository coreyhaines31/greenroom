Mac guide · macOS 14 Sonoma and later

AirPods not connecting to your Mac? Fix it in order.

Most of the time they're busy with your iPhone, low on charge, or need to be paired again. Start at the top.

Quick check first: put your AirPods in your ears, click Control Center in the menu bar, then Sound. If they're in the list, pick them and play the free sound test. If they aren't, keep going.

Connect AirPods to a Mac, step by step

  1. Make sure Bluetooth is on.Choose Apple menu › System Settings › Bluetooth and turn it on.
  2. Charge both AirPods.Put both in the charging case and make sure they're charging. A dead case or one flat AirPod is a common reason only one side connects, or neither does.
  3. Pick them as your output.With the AirPods in your ears, click Control Center in the menu bar, click Sound, and choose your AirPods. You can also click Connect next to them in System Settings › Bluetooth.
  4. Give them a fresh start.Put both AirPods in the case and close the lid. Wait 15 seconds, open the lid, and try to connect again.
  5. Check your macOS version.Newer AirPods need a newer Mac system. AirPods 4 need macOS Sequoia or later, and AirPods Pro 2 with USB-C need macOS Sonoma or later. Check for updates in System Settings › General › Software Update.
  6. Pair them again.In System Settings, click your AirPods' name in the sidebar, then Forget This Device. Put both AirPods in the case with the lid open. Press and hold the setup button on the back of the case until the light flashes white (on AirPods 4, AirPods 5, and AirPods Pro 3, double-tap the front of the case instead). Then click Connect next to them under Nearby Devices in System Settings › Bluetooth.
  7. Reset the AirPods.Put them in the case, close the lid, and wait 30 seconds. Open the lid, then hold the setup button for about 15 seconds until the light flashes amber, then white. On AirPods 4, AirPods 5, and AirPods Pro 3, double-tap the front of the case three times instead. Then pair them again as in step 6.
  8. Restart your Mac.If the AirPods connect to your phone but never to your Mac, a restart clears a stuck Bluetooth connection.

They keep jumping to your iPhone?

AirPods signed in to your Apple Account move to whichever device you're using. To make them stay put, open System Settings › Bluetooth, click the info button next to your AirPods, and under Connect to This Mac choose When last connected to this Mac.

Connected, but no sound?

Your Mac may still be playing through its speakers or a display. Open Control Center › Sound and choose the AirPods. Call apps like Zoom keep their own speaker choice, so check the app's audio settings too. The headphone test confirms both sides work.

What Green Room does automatically

Green Room is a free menu bar app that keeps an eye on your camera, mic, and speakers, including AirPods.

  • Says why they're missing. When AirPods you usually use aren't there, the panel says Not connected and tells you to turn them on or connect them in Bluetooth settings.
  • Switches back when AirPods take over. If AirPods coming out of the case grab your sound in the middle of your day, it can switch back to your usual speakers and tell you.
  • Catches call mode. When a call app turns on the AirPods mic and everything drops to phone quality, it tells you and offers your Mac's own mic.

On a Mac? Green Room tells you why your AirPods aren't there.

It knows your usual devices, so a missing pair shows up with the reason and the fix. Free, with no account.
